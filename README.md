# financial-performance-ratio-analysis
An end-to-end financial analytics project analyzing multi-year financial statements of publicly traded companies. The project evaluates revenue growth, profitability, liquidity, leverage, cash-flow generation, ROA, ROE, and other financial ratios using Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Excel.
Financial Performance vs. Stock Returns

The correlation analysis was used to examine the linear relationships between selected financial performance indicators and annual stock returns. The results show relatively weak relationships between the accounting measures and stock returns within the analyzed sample.

Revenue Growth had a correlation of -0.04 with Annual Stock Return, indicating an almost negligible linear relationship. Net Margin had the strongest positive correlation with Annual Stock Return at 0.17, although this still represents a weak relationship. ROE had a correlation of -0.10, while Free Cash Flow had a correlation of -0.12.

These results suggest that the selected financial indicators did not show a strong linear relationship with annual stock returns in this particular sample. This should not be interpreted as evidence that financial performance is irrelevant to stock prices. Stock returns are influenced by many factors, including investor expectations, market conditions, valuation, interest rates, industry developments, and company-specific events. In addition, the financial data covers a limited period and the number of observations used for the correlation analysis is relatively small.

The analysis also identified stronger relationships among some of the financial variables themselves. Net Margin and Free Cash Flow had a correlation of 0.75, indicating a relatively strong positive linear relationship in the sample. Revenue Growth and Free Cash Flow had a correlation of 0.44, representing a moderate positive relationship.

Overall, the correlation analysis demonstrates that accounting performance indicators and stock-market returns can behave differently. Financial ratios provide useful information about a company's operating and financial condition, but they should not be treated as standalone predictors of stock returns.
